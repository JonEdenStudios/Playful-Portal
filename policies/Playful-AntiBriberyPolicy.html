01

Policy statement

Playful Ltd has a zero-tolerance approach to bribery and corruption. We are committed to acting with integrity in all our business dealings and relationships, and to implementing and enforcing effective procedures to counter bribery wherever it might arise.

This policy applies to all Playful employees, contractors, Playmakers and anyone acting on Playful’s behalf — including international collaborators and distribution partners. It applies regardless of where in the world business is conducted.

Playful Ltd is subject to the UK Bribery Act 2010. Under this legislation it is a criminal offence to offer, promise, give, request or accept a bribe. The corporate offence under Section 7 of the Act means Playful can be held liable if a person associated with the business bribes another person on our behalf — even without the knowledge of the founders. This policy forms part of our defence against that risk.

  • All collaborators, agents and distribution partners acting on behalf of Playful — including international partners such as those within the Timberplay worldwide network and US-based collaborators such as Cas Holman

04

What counts as bribery

A bribe is a financial or other advantage offered, promised or given to induce someone to perform their function improperly, or to reward them for having done so. This includes:

  • Cash payments, gifts, hospitality or entertainment offered to obtain or retain business or a commercial advantage
  • Payments made through third parties, agents or intermediaries where the ultimate purpose is improper
  • Facilitation payments — small unofficial payments to speed up routine actions by a public official. These are illegal under the Bribery Act regardless of local custom or practice, and Playful will not make or authorise them

Bribery can occur in the private sector and the public sector, and in the UK and overseas. It does not require a corrupt intention on both sides — the offer alone can constitute an offence.

05

Gifts and hospitality

Playful recognises that reasonable and proportionate gifts and hospitality are a normal part of business relationships. This policy does not prohibit them. The test is whether a gift or hospitality could reasonably be seen as an attempt to improperly influence a business decision.

Acceptable

  • Modest gifts of low value in the normal course of a business relationship
  • Reasonable hospitality — a working lunch, an industry event, a site visit meal — where the primary purpose is business

Not acceptable

  • Gifts of significant value, particularly around the time of a commercial decision
  • Hospitality that is lavish, frequent or personal in nature
  • Cash or cash equivalents in any amount
  • Anything given to a public official that could be interpreted as influencing their official duties

If you are unsure whether a gift or hospitality is appropriate, speak to Dirk before accepting or offering it. When in doubt, decline.

06

Third parties and international business

The greatest bribery risk for Playful arises from our international connections. Where we work with agents, distribution partners or collaborators outside the UK — including through the Timberplay worldwide network and with US-based collaborators — we are potentially exposed to the Section 7 corporate offence if those parties engage in bribery on our behalf.

Before entering into any significant new third-party relationship with an international dimension, Dirk will carry out proportionate due diligence, which may include:

  • Understanding who the third party is and who they are connected to
  • Assessing the level of bribery risk in the relevant country or sector
  • Ensuring the third party is aware of and committed to our anti-bribery position
  • Including appropriate anti-bribery representations in any formal agreement

Facilitation payments requested by overseas partners or agents are not authorised under any circumstances.

07

Reporting concerns

If you become aware of — or suspect — any bribery, corruption or breach of this policy, report it to Dirk Mischendahl as soon as possible. If the concern involves Dirk, report it to Jon Eden or Beth Cooper.

Reports will be treated confidentially and investigated promptly. Playful will not penalise anyone who raises a genuine concern in good faith, even if it turns out to be unfounded.

Failure to report a known breach is itself a serious matter.

08

Consequences of breach

Any breach of this policy by an employee will be treated as gross misconduct. Any breach by a contractor or third party will result in the immediate review of that relationship and may result in termination.

Individuals found to have engaged in bribery may also face personal criminal liability under the Bribery Act 2010.

09

Record keeping

Playful will keep records of any gifts or hospitality given or received where there is any question of appropriateness, and of any due diligence carried out on third-party relationships. These records will be maintained by Dirk Mischendahl and made available if required.

10

Policy review

This policy will be reviewed annually, or sooner following any significant change in Playful’s business activities — particularly those involving new international relationships.
