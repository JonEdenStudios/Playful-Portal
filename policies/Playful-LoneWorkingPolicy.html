01

Policy statement

Playful Ltd recognises that lone working is a regular part of how we operate. Our founders and contractors frequently work alone — from home, during site visits, at installations and events, and at client meetings. We are committed to ensuring that lone working is carried out safely and that everyone working alone on Playful’s behalf is appropriately supported.

This policy sits alongside the Health & Safety Policy and supplements it with specific guidance for lone working situations.

02

Scope

This policy covers:

  • All three Playful founders and employees when working alone
  • All contractors and Playmakers working alone on Playful projects or in Playful contexts
  • All lone working settings: home offices, site visits, installations, events, client meetings and travel
03

What is lone working

For the purposes of this policy, lone working means any situation where a person is working without close or direct supervision and without immediate access to colleagues — whether at home, at a venue, on site or in transit.

This includes but is not limited to:

  • Working from home during normal working hours
  • Travelling to and from sites or client meetings
  • Carrying out site surveys or venue visits alone
  • Setting up or breaking down Playful installations without a colleague present
  • Staffing events or activations alone
04

Responsibilities

Beth Cooper is the named Health & Safety lead and holds responsibility for this policy. All employees, contractors and Playmakers are responsible for following the procedures in this policy when working alone and for reporting any concerns or incidents promptly.

05

Before lone working

Before undertaking any lone working activity — particularly site visits, installations or events — employees and contractors should:

  • Inform a colleague, co-founder or trusted contact of their location and expected return time
  • Ensure they have a fully charged mobile phone
  • Have the contact details of the venue or site manager where relevant
  • Carry out a brief personal risk assessment: is the environment familiar? Are there any known hazards? Is there a reason not to proceed alone?

For higher-risk activities — such as setting up a Playwall installation or working in an unfamiliar public space — a second person should be arranged wherever possible.

06

During lone working

While working alone, employees and contractors should:

  • Keep their mobile phone accessible and charged
  • Check in with a colleague or contact if the work is extended or the situation changes
  • Not attempt manual handling tasks that require two people
  • Trust their instincts — if a situation feels unsafe, stop and seek assistance
  • Follow all venue or site safety rules as they apply to lone workers
07

Check-in procedure

For site visits, installations and events lasting more than a few hours, a simple check-in arrangement should be agreed in advance:

  • Confirm a check-in time with a colleague or co-founder before leaving
  • Make contact at the agreed time to confirm all is well
  • If contact is not made within a reasonable period of the agreed time, the colleague should attempt to reach the lone worker by phone and, if unsuccessful, take appropriate action including contacting the venue or emergency services if necessary

A text message confirming safe arrival and departure is sufficient for most situations. It does not need to be formal — it just needs to happen.

08

Returning home safely

After any lone working activity away from home, employees and contractors should confirm safe return to a colleague or contact where a check-in arrangement has been made.

09

Accidents and incidents

If emergency assistance is required, call 999 first.

Any accident, injury or security incident that occurs during lone working must be reported to Beth Cooper as soon as practicable.

10

Policy review

This policy will be reviewed annually, or sooner following any incident involving a lone worker or significant change in working practice.
