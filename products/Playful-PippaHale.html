Background

Pippa Hale is a contemporary British artist based in Leeds. She is the founder of the Northern Art Prize and co-founder of Leeds contemporary art gallery The Tetley. She stepped down as co-director of The Tetley in 2015 and joined the board of trustees of its management company, Leeds Project Space.

Her practice centres around history, geography and play and includes large-scale installations in heritage venues, the public realm and museums and galleries in media ranging from foam and inflatables to iron and digital technology. She is interested in the parallels between creativity and play and likes to make her artworks — not just playful — but playable. Wherever possible she roots artworks to their locations, often working with local communities to connect people to places.

She is a member of Artists’ Union England and the Royal Society of Sculptors.

Playshapes was directly inspired by time spent in soft play centres with her own children. It was born out of frustration with the static, fixed, primary-coloured equipment that allowed limited play opportunities — and aimed to open up opportunities for kids and adults to create their own playful environments with sophisticated shapes and colours. The design draws on the work of Isamu Noguchi and Froebel’s Gifts as a way of introducing people to experiment with colour and three-dimensional form.

02

Who she is

Pippa Hale is an artist and play designer, and one of Playful’s closest creative collaborators. She is the designer of both Playshapes and Playwall — Playful’s two flagship physical products — and the creative lead on any bespoke or custom work that sits within that family.

Playshapes has its own product website at www.playshapes.co.uk.

Pippa’s practice sits at the intersection of art, design and play. Her work is characterised by strong visual identity, open-ended engagement and a commitment to play that works across ages — children and adults alike.

Bespoke and custom work

Bespoke designs are available in direct collaboration with Pippa. This covers:

  • Custom shapes — new cube types or modified forms outside the standard Playshapes set
  • Custom colourways — colours outside the standard 21-colour palette, or matched to a venue’s brand
  • New product development — entirely new play objects or systems, developed as a commission

Bespoke work is quoted individually and agreed directly with Pippa. Lead times will be longer than standard product orders. Position the bespoke route for clients with a genuine brief for something unique, or where the standard range doesn’t fit the space or the brand.

05

Key notes for proposals

  • The artist-designed provenance is a genuine differentiator in cultural venue contexts. The fact that Playshapes and Playwall come from a named artist — not a manufacturer’s catalogue — matters to commissioners who care about the creative story.
  • Always name Pippa in proposals where her work is featured. She is part of what makes the products distinctive.
  • For bespoke enquiries, bring Pippa into the conversation early. The most successful bespoke projects start with a proper creative brief, not a modified standard order.
