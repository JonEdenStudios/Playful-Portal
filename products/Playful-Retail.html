What it is

Playful offers a play-led retail consultancy service to cultural venues, heritage sites and visitor attractions. The brief is straightforward: apply play theory and creative insight to the retail environment so that families stay longer, spend more and want to come back.

This is not about filling a shop with toys. It is about understanding what the venue has already given visitors — the stories, the drama, the emotional experience — and making sure the retail space extends that rather than replacing it with something generic.

Most heritage and cultural shops are designed as endpoints. A playful retail approach treats the shop as the last chapter of the visit. That shift in thinking changes everything.

02

What it covers

  • Retail audit and opportunity mapping — a structured look at the current offer set against what the visitor experience sets up.
  • Play-theory insight — applying developmental play knowledge to product selection as a practical filter. Does this product earn its place?
  • Product direction and curation — identifying product directions and supplier categories that connect to the venue’s story.
  • Space thinking — the physical retail environment alongside the product mix. Small changes here often make the biggest difference.
  • Family audience expertise — understanding the different needs within a family visit. A five-year-old and a twelve-year-old are having different experiences.
  • Commercial grounding — every recommendation is considered against margin, operational reality and what the client can practically deliver.
03

The commercial mechanism

The commercial logic runs in four steps:

01

Curiosity

A space that asks questions instead of displaying products. Something to touch or discover draws visitors in.

02

Exploration

Curious visitors move differently. They pick things up, they read, they bring their children over. Time in the space increases.

03

Dwell time

Engaged families stay longer. Dwell time is the clearest predictor of spend.

04

Secondary spend

Products connected to the visit experience have higher perceived value and lower purchase resistance. Castle-specific beats generic.

04

What it’s suited to

Good fit

  • Heritage sites and cultural venues with an underperforming retail offer
  • Organisations post-refurbishment or at a point of strategic review
  • Venues with a strong visitor experience not reflected in the shop
  • Family-facing attractions where dwell time and secondary spend are priorities
  • Organisations wanting to grow family revenue without major capital investment

Less suited

  • Venues with no retail operation or no interest in developing one
  • Purely commercial retail environments without a cultural anchor
05

How it’s delivered

Typically as a written thinking piece, strategy document or recommendations report — shaped to the client’s decision-making context. Can include space layouts, product direction frameworks or audience analysis depending on scope.

Playful brings three lenses: Discovery (understanding the place, the visitors and what already works), Storytelling (extending the venue’s narrative into the retail offer), and Making (drawing on Playful’s network of artists, designers and fabricators where a physical intervention is part of the answer).

06

Key notes for proposals

  • Lead with the commercial case. Dwell time, secondary spend and repeat visitation are the language that gets budget approved.
  • The “last chapter” framing is effective with heritage and cultural clients — it respects the primacy of the experience while making the case for retail.
  • The Solid Wobble™ positioning works well here: Playful arrives with a way of thinking, not a catalogue.
  • The Caerphilly piece was positioned as a thinking piece rather than a pitch — that tone opened the conversation without creating procurement pressure. Worth replicating.
  • Retail consultancy can sit alongside a product sale (Playshapes, Mini Playbox, Playwall) or stand independently. Also a natural follow-on from a feasibility study.

Past projects

Caerphilly Castle × CADW, April 2026

Play-led retail thinking piece for Louise Hirst at CADW, focused on the gift shop at Caerphilly Castle — Wales’s largest castle and recently refurbished. The document set out six castle-specific thinking directions: the leaning tower, water and siege engineering, Welsh myth and legend, heraldry and identity, medieval craft, and Gilbert’s Maze. Presented by Beth Cooper and Dirk Mischendahl.
